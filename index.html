About This Site

Diwa Pilipinas is a student showcase gallery built with HTML5, CSS3, and Bootstrap 5. It highlights 12 living traditions across the Philippines' three major island groups — Luzon, Visayas, and Mindanao — spanning festivals, attire, cuisine, and craftsmanship, using a fully responsive grid, an interactive gallery, and a details modal for each entry.

Built as a Midterm Laboratory project for CCS114 – Web Technologies, University of Cabuyao.

Barong Tagalog

Nationwide, rooted in Luzon — Attire

Made of sheer, embroidered fabric like piña or jusi, the Barong Tagalog is the traditional formal wear for Filipino men, worn at weddings, graduations, and state functions.

Filipiniana / Terno

Nationwide, rooted in Luzon — Attire

Known for its structured butterfly sleeves, the Filipiniana is the elegant, gauzy dress that has represented Filipino womanhood since the Spanish colonial era.

Pahiyas Festival

Lucban, Quezon — Luzon

Held every May, Pahiyas sees homes in Lucban decorated top to bottom with colorful rice wafers called kiping and farm produce, giving thanks for a bountiful harvest.

Adobo

Nationwide, rooted in Luzon — Food

Widely regarded as the country's unofficial national dish, adobo is meat slow-simmered in vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, and pepper — a method born from preserving food in a tropical climate.

Baybayin Script

Pre-colonial, widespread in Luzon — Arts & Crafts

An ancient syllabic writing system used across the islands before Spanish colonization, Baybayin has become a modern symbol of pride in indigenous Filipino heritage.

Parol

San Fernando, Pampanga — Luzon

This star-shaped lantern lights up Filipino homes every Christmas season, symbolizing hope and guidance, with the giant lanterns of Pampanga among the most famous in the country.

Sinulog Festival

Cebu City — Visayas

One of the grandest festivals in the country, Sinulog honors the Santo Niño with a week of street dancing, drumbeats, and processions drawing millions of devotees each January.

Ati-Atihan Festival

Kalibo, Aklan — Visayas

Often called the "Mother of All Philippine Festivals," Ati-Atihan features soot-painted revelers dancing to tribal drums in a joyous, chaotic tribute to the Santo Niño.

Lechon

Cebu — Visayas

Whole pig roasted slowly over charcoal until the skin turns crackling-crisp, lechon is the celebratory centerpiece of Filipino feasts, with Cebu's version famed for needing no sauce.

Tinikling

Leyte origin, danced nationwide — Visayas

The Philippines' best-known folk dance mimics the movement of the tikling bird as dancers nimbly step between two bamboo poles clashed together in rhythm.

Kulintang

Maguindanao — Mindanao

A row of small, graduated gongs played in intricate rhythms, the kulintang ensemble is central to the indigenous musical traditions of Mindanao's Muslim and Lumad communities.

T'nalak Weaving

South Cotabato — Mindanao

Woven from abaca fiber by the T'boli "dreamweavers," t'nalak cloth carries patterns said to be revealed to the weaver in her dreams, making each piece sacred and unrepeatable.
